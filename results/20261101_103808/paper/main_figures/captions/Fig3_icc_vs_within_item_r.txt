Figure 3. Agreement across cells versus sensitivity to the images.
Each point is one model (colour and shape = provider). y = ICC(2,1) over the 200 cells, which rewards getting the item profile right (e.g. that "interested" is rated higher than "hostile" in every scene). x = within-item r: the pooled correlation over the 200 cells after subtracting, for humans and the model separately, each item's mean over the ten scenes. It therefore measures only whether the model agrees on which scenes score higher or lower on a given item, and ignores the item profile.
The dashed horizontal line (ICC 0.57) is a scene-blind baseline that predicts every scene with the human mean of the item; it uses no image information at all and is evaluated in-sample, which flatters it. Horizontal bands are the tiers (low < .70, mid .70-.80, top >= .80).
Across the 43 models ICC ranges 0.56-0.87 (median 0.79) and within-item r 0.63-0.90 (median 0.84). The two are related (Spearman 0.73 across models) but not interchangeable: qwen3.8-27b and gemini-3.5-flash-lite have an ICC at the baseline level (0.55-0.57) yet a within-item r of 0.82, above the median of the models' ICC-matched peers, so they respond to the images but get item levels or scale use wrong; gemini-2.5-flash is low on both (0.57, 0.63). Labelled points: the top model (Gemini 4 Argon), models at or near the baseline level, and selected others discussed in the text.

Units and conventions. Units throughout: 200 image x item cells (10 environments x 20 PANAS items); N = 84 participants; 10 independent runs per model and image; 43 models (two aliased Gemini models excluded). ICC = ICC(2,1), two-way random, absolute agreement, between the human cell means and the model cell means. Tiers: low < .70, mid .70-.80, top >= .80.
