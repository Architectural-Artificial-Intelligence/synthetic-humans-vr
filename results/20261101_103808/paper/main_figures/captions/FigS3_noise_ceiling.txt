Figure S3. How well can anything agree with an individual?
Blue histogram: for each of the 84 participants, the correlation over the 200 item x scene cells between that participant's ratings and the mean of all 83 others (median 0.69, mean 0.66; range 0.06-0.85). Black line: median. Orange ticks along the bottom: for each model, its mean correlation with the individual participants (one tick per model); the best model (Gemini 4 Argon) reaches a mean r of 0.60.
The individual-versus-others correlation is a noise ceiling: it is how well the average person's ratings can predict a single person's. The lower and upper bounds (leave-one-out vs including the participant) are 0.656 and 0.665. No model's mean correlation with individuals exceeds the lower bound (0 of 43), i.e. models are better predictors of the group mean than of any person, but not better than another human would be. High agreement with the group mean (Figure 5) therefore does not imply that a model reproduces individual responses.

Units and conventions. Units throughout: 200 image x item cells (10 environments x 20 PANAS items); N = 84 participants; 10 independent runs per model and image; 43 models (two aliased Gemini models excluded). ICC = ICC(2,1), two-way random, absolute agreement, between the human cell means and the model cell means. Tiers: low < .70, mid .70-.80, top >= .80.
