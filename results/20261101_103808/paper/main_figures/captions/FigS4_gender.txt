Figure S4. Women and men rate positive affect alike; women report more negative affect in some environments.
(a, b) Environment-level PA and NA means for women (filled, n = 55) and men (hollow, n = 29) with 95% CIs; environments sorted by overall human NA. Asterisks: Welch test, Benjamini–Hochberg FDR within subscale (* < .05, ** < .01). (c) ICC(2,1) of each of the 43 models with the men's and with the women's 200 cell means; women subsampled to n = 29 so both references are equally reliable (mean over 2,000 subsamples). Points below the dashed identity line agree more with men.
Participant-level PA did not differ (g = 0.00, p = .989) and was equivalent within d = ±0.5 (TOST p = .019, BF01 = 4.2). Women reported higher NA (+0.24 points, g = 0.54 [0.13, 0.97], Holm p = .023, BF10 = 2.6). The NA difference varied by environment (gender × environment F(9, 738) = 3.71, Greenhouse–Geisser p = .001) and was significant only in Service car park (g = 0.87) and Parking hall (g = 0.65). 43 of 43 models agreed more with men (mean ΔICC = -0.106 [-0.171, -0.016]), but within-item r did not differ (Δ = -0.041 [-0.086, +0.040]): the gap reflects the level of NA, which models under-rate and men rate lower, not the pattern across environments. Exploratory analysis; full results in Tables S11–S12.
