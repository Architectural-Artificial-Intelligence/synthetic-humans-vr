Figure 2. Do models track which environments feel better or worse?
Each point is one environment (numbers are scene numbers as in Figure 1). x = human mean of the scale (84 participants, averaged over the 10 PA or 10 NA items); y = mean over 10 runs of the same scale from Gemini 4 Argon, the highest-ICC model (blue). Left: positive affect; right: negative affect. The dashed diagonal is identity.
The grey vertical bar at each x shows the spread of the same environment across all 43 models: thick = interquartile range, thin = min-max. A bar sitting below the diagonal means models rate that environment lower than humans do.
Text in each panel gives the slope of the model mean on the human mean across the ten environments. For PA the median model slope is 0.88 (Argon 1.05); for NA it is 0.60 (Argon 0.70). A slope below 1 means models compress differences between environments, most strongly for negative affect: they separate the bad environments (3, 5, 9, 10) from the good ones, but by roughly 60% of the human difference. Models also sit below identity for NA, i.e. they under-rate negative affect (see Figure 4).

Units and conventions. Units throughout: 200 image x item cells (10 environments x 20 PANAS items); N = 84 participants; 10 independent runs per model and image; 43 models (two aliased Gemini models excluded). ICC = ICC(2,1), two-way random, absolute agreement, between the human cell means and the model cell means. Tiers: low < .70, mid .70-.80, top >= .80.
