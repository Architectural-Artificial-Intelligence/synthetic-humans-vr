Figure 4. Which items do models mis-rate?
Signed error = model minus human, in rating-scale points, per item: the mean over all 10 environments and all runs, so negative values mean the model rates the item lower than humans do. Each row summarises the 43 models: marker = median, thick bar = interquartile range, thin bar = min-max. Top panel: the ten positive-affect items; bottom panel: the ten negative-affect items; each sorted by median error. The vertical line at 0 is no bias. Diamonds mark the three items that were adapted for the Hebrew PANAS (hostile, jittery, distressed).
Negative affect is under-rated almost everywhere: the median error is below zero for 9 of the 10 NA items, and 39 of 43 models under-rate NA overall. The largest under-rating is "hostile" (median -0.91, all 43 models below zero), followed by "upset", "afraid", "nervous" and "scared" (-0.44 to -0.58). "Irritable" is the only NA item near zero (+0.11). PA errors are smaller and less consistent: "active", "alert" and "determined" are under-rated, "proud" is about zero.
Items are not independent of the scene-level result in Figure 2: a uniform downward shift on NA is what compresses the model-on-human slope there.

Units and conventions. Units throughout: 200 image x item cells (10 environments x 20 PANAS items); N = 84 participants; 10 independent runs per model and image; 43 models (two aliased Gemini models excluded). ICC = ICC(2,1), two-way random, absolute agreement, between the human cell means and the model cell means. Tiers: low < .70, mid .70-.80, top >= .80.
