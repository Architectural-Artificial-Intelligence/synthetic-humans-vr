# Methods — Stimuli, VR procedure and instrument (draft v2)

## Stimuli

Ten real urban environments were photographed as 360° panoramas (equirectangular, 3584 × 1792 px, JPEG) with a handheld smartphone (Xiaomi 23090RA98G) on a single afternoon (9 March 2025, 14:55–15:58), so that daylight and weather were comparable across scenes. Scenes were chosen by the authors to differ clearly in spatial enclosure, luminance, vegetation, activity and maintenance, and comprise outdoor streets and public spaces, indoor commercial spaces and below-ground service spaces (Supplementary Table 1). All were captured from standing eye height at pedestrian positions. Because the panoramas were stitched on the device, the photographer's feet are visible at the nadir and minor stitching artefacts are visible in two scenes (3 and 4); these were identical for human participants and VLMs.

## Participants

Eighty-four adults took part (55 women, 29 men; age M = 26.7, SD = 7.2, range 18–58). This was a convenience sample: architecture students who received course credit, other students, faculty members, and friends and family of the researchers. Seventy-one participants (84.5%) reported an architecture-related occupation or field of study; the sample therefore represents a design-literate population rather than the general public. All had normal or corrected-to-normal vision and gave informed consent; the study was approved by the institutional ethics committee [committee name and approval number]. Data were collected between May 2025 and January 2026. The survey export contains 84 complete records (one test entry with no PANAS responses was removed before analysis) and no missing PANAS responses; no imputation was required. No a-priori power analysis was performed; precision of the human reference is reported instead (split-half reliability of the 200 cell means, Spearman–Brown 0.985).

## VR procedure

Sessions took place individually in a quiet laboratory without other auditory or visual stimuli. Panoramas were presented as monoscopic 360° images on a Meta Quest 2 head-mounted display. Participants were seated on a swivel chair and did not move through space; they could look in all directions by turning the head and the chair (three rotational degrees of freedom). After a safety briefing and consent, participants were instructed to take their time to view each place, to feel as if they were there, and then to report how the space made them feel. Each scene was viewed for about one minute. Participants then answered the PANAS orally while still wearing the headset (about one minute); a research assistant recorded the answers together with the image number. The ten scenes were shown in an individually randomised order generated by a script before each session. Participants kept the headset on throughout; short pauses with the headset removed may have occurred but were not recorded.

## Instrument

Affect was measured with the 20-item PANAS (Watson et al., 1988) in its validated Hebrew version (Ben-Zur, 2002; α = .82 in the validation sample). For this study the items were adapted to the environmental state context: the instruction asked how the participant felt now, and each item was phrased as the extent to which "the space makes you feel" the emotion, rated on a five-point scale (1 = not at all, 5 = very much). Three wordings depart from Ben-Zur's version: *distressed* was presented as "stressed" (לחוץ/ה) rather than "in distress" (נתון/ה במצוקה); *jittery* as "a sense of tension" (תחושת מתח) rather than "tense" (מתוח/ה); and *hostile* was phrased as an appraisal of the space ("how hostile does the space feel to you") rather than of the participant's own feeling. Items were mapped to the English PANAS items following Ben-Zur (2002). ## VLM procedure (summary; full details in Supplementary Table T1)

VLMs received the same ten equirectangular panoramas used in the headset, unmodified, as base64-encoded JPEGs (3584 × 1792 px; providers may downscale internally). Human participants therefore saw a navigable, head-tracked view of each scene, whereas models received the complete scene as a single flat projection in one image: the visual content is the same, but its presentation differs (projection distortion, no head-tracked viewing, no sense of scale or presence). The prompt (reproduced in full in Box 1) asked the model to act as an environmental psychologist and to simulate the average PANAS ratings that 20 typical people would give for how the environment would make them feel. It therefore elicits a predicted group mean, not the model's own affective state. Each model rated each image ten times in independent calls at temperature 0.7 where the API accepted it ([n] models ran at the provider default because they reject custom temperatures), with a distinct seed per call where supported. Every call was logged (raw response, returned model version, temperature, seed, tokens, timestamp). All calls were made on 13 October 2026.
